# Sovereign Intelligence Mapping

Perform motive analysis and behavioral pattern detection on information
sources to understand their reliability and incentives.

## Core Concept

A source's reliability depends on three factors:
1. **What they have skin in the game** — material interest in outcomes
2. **What they have proven to do** — historical behavior, not stated mission
3. **What happens when they tell the truth at a cost** — integrity test

## Motive Analysis Framework

### Layer 1: Material Interest
- Does the source (or its funders) stand to gain financially/politically
  from a particular outcome?
- Are they positioned to profit from the problem they're "reporting"?
- Do their funders have stakes in the industries or policies they cover?

### Layer 2: Behavioral Consistency
- Does the source apply the same standards to ideologically aligned and
  opposed subjects?
- Do they report inconvenient truths about their own coalition?
- What happens when a story threatens their funders' interests?

### Layer 3: Integrity Signals
- **Truth at cost:** Has the source ever reported something true that
  harmed its funders or coalition? This is the strongest integrity signal.
- **Truth at profit:** Does the source only report truths when they serve
  their interests? This suggests instrumental truth-telling.

## Linguistic Marker Detection

Identify shibboleths — words or phrases that signal ideological alignment:
- Track which terms a source uses vs. avoids
- Note euphemisms and loaded language
- Cross-reference with known ideological marker databases

## Institutional Capture Detection

Signs of institutional capture:
1. **Revolving door:** Regulators become industry consultants, journalists
   become PR representatives for their subjects
2. **Self-association:** Organizations name themselves after beneficiaries
   ("World Health Organization") while serving funders
3. **Problem-solution complex:** The people profiting from the problem
   control the "solutions"
4. **Access journalism:** Favorable coverage in exchange for access to
   powerful people

## Output

Create or update a source dossier with motive analysis sections.
See `web-source-bias-research` skill for dossier format.

## Critical Rules

1. Motives are inferred from behavior, not statements
2. A source can be reliable on some topics and unreliable on others
3. The goal is not to dismiss sources but to understand their incentives
4. Document the evidence chain — how you got from observation to conclusion
